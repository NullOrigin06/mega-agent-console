Heat exchanger drawings, straight from the datasheet.

Mega Engineering Suite takes the numbers you already have (shell ID, tube size and pitch, nozzle schedule) and produces the fabrication drawing, the general arrangement and the bill of materials. It covers shell-and-tube exchangers, tube sheets, bonnet flanges and storage tanks.

Every part on the drawing is a line in the BOM.

Heads, bonnet flanges, studs, tube sheets, baffles and the tube bundle are separate items in the model. Each one lands in the bill of materials with its size, material and weight, so nothing gets counted by hand.

Sample exchanger shown: shell ID 800 mm, tube sheet diameter 925 mm with 420 holes, tubes 25.4 mm on a 31.75 mm triangular pitch, 3,008 mm long, five baffles at 483 mm pitch with a 25% cut, bonnet flanges with 28 stud bolts per end, two saddles at 1,240 mm centres, 4,090 mm overall.

Tube side and shell side, laid out by rule.

With the shell pulled back you can see what the layout rules produce. This sample is an 800 mm shell with 420 tubes on a 31.75 mm triangular pitch, and five baffles cut at 25% so the shell-side flow crosses the bundle.

The moving dots are only an illustration. The suite draws exchangers. It does not simulate them.

Then it becomes a general arrangement.

Overall length, tube length, baffle pitch and saddle centres are dimensioned from the same inputs as the model, so the drawing and the bill of materials agree. The DWG is made in GstarCAD or AutoCAD on your own workstation.

What you see here is a sketch of that drawing, not the DWG itself.
